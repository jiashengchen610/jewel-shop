# Writing guidelines

Shared by every brand. These guidelines are for a budgeting dashboard for households: income, bills, savings goals and spending limits in one place. The words should make money feel organized and manageable, for everyone in the household.

## Voice

Clear, calm and specific. We sound like a level-headed person who keeps the household's paperwork in order: plain words, exact numbers, no drama. The voice stays the same everywhere; the tone adapts to the moment.

- Clear: lead with the number or the fact, then the detail. One idea per sentence.
- Calm: state what happened without alarm or judgment. Money can be stressful; the copy should not add to it.
- Specific: exact amounts, dates and names. "Rent, $1,200, due Friday" beats "Upcoming payment".
- Inclusive: write for the whole household. Say "your household" or name the person, never assume one earner.

## Tone by situation

| Situation | Tone |
|---|---|
| Onboarding, empty states | Encouraging and direct: say what will appear here and the one next step |
| Over a limit, low balance | Neutral and helpful: the facts and an option, never blame |
| Goals reached, bills paid | Quietly positive: a short acknowledgement is enough |
| Reminders and notifications | Brief and useful: what is due, when, and how much |
| Errors | Calm and exact: what happened and how to fix it; no apologies |
| Destructive confirmations | Serious and explicit about what will be lost |

## Interface copy rules

- Buttons start with a verb and say what happens: "Add bill", "Save goal", "Set limit", not "Submit" or "OK".
- An action keeps its name through the flow: "Add bill" leads to "Bill added".
- Sentence case for all labels, headings and buttons.
- Card titles name the topic in 1 to 3 words: "Bills this month", "Savings goals".
- Error messages name the problem and the fix in one or two short sentences: "This date is in the past. Pick a due date from today on."
- Use numerals and currency symbols: "$45 left", "3 bills due this week".
- Show progress as amounts: "$320 of $500 spent", "$1,800 saved of $5,000".
- Say over-limit plainly: "$40 over your groceries limit", not "You've overspent!".
- Offer options, not orders: "Move $40 from Eating out?" instead of "You should cut back".

## Terminology

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Income | Inflows, earnings (in labels) |
| Bill | Payable, obligation |
| Spending | Expenditure, outgoings |
| Spending limit | Cap, allowance, budget ceiling |
| Savings goal | Target, savings objective |
| Left to spend | Remaining balance, available funds |
| Over limit | Overspent, in the red |
| Household | Account group, workspace |
| Sign in | Log in, Login |
| Delete | Remove (when the item is gone for good) |

## Do's and Don'ts

- Do write from the household's side: "Your rent is due Friday", not "Scheduled payment pending".
- Do explain a money term in plain words the first time it appears.
- Don't moralize about spending or compare households to others.
- Don't use finance jargon or bank-speak.
- Don't use exclamation marks in errors or money warnings; use them rarely anywhere.
- Don't use filler like "simply" or "just".
